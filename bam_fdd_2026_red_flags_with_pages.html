Franchisor's own FDD questions its financial ability to support franchisees

Critical

Primary trademark is NOT on the principal register — only the supplemental register

"Bricks & Minifigs" has been on the supplemental register since 2012 — a significantly weaker form of protection. The FDD states: "Registration on the supplemental register provides more limited legal protections than registration on the principal register. As a result, if your right to use the trademark is challenged, you may be required to change to an alternative trademark." Principal register applications were only filed in July 2023 and are still pending — over 3 years later. All 224 franchisees are operating under a brand with potentially vulnerable IP.

Negative stockholders' equity — ($621K) — technically insolvent on paper

Total liabilities of $6.69M exceed total assets of $6.07M. The deficit has worsened every year: ($182K) in 2022 → ($277K) in 2023 → ($493K) in 2024 → ($621K) in 2025. The company has never had positive equity across the entire history presented. This is the core driver of the mandatory financial condition risk disclosure above.

Significant — contractual and structural concerns
Significant

Mandatory minimum royalties regardless of sales — termination risk if you can't pay

Royalties are 6% of gross revenue or $500/month minimum, whichever is greater. Brand fund adds another 1%, local marketing requires 3%, and a technology fund of up to 1% may be added. Total mandatory fee burden could reach 11% of revenue before technology fund. The FDD explicitly warns: "Your inability to make the payments may result in termination of your franchise and loss of your investment."

Special Risks #2 (p. 5) Item 6 — Other Fees (p. 15)
Significant

All disputes must be resolved in Utah — regardless of where your store is located

Arbitration, mediation, and litigation are all required in Utah County, Utah under Utah law. The FDD itself flags this as a special risk for out-of-state buyers: it "may force you to accept a less favorable settlement" and "may cost more to mediate, arbitrate, or litigate." For a franchisee in Florida or New York this is a meaningful barrier to pursuing any legal remedy.

Special Risks #1 (p. 5) Item 17(u)–(v) (p. 58)
Significant

3-year post-termination non-compete, including worldwide e-commerce restriction

After the franchise ends, you cannot compete within 25 miles of any company-owned or franchised location for 3 years. More unusually, there is also a 3-year ban on any interest in a competing business "via online e-commerce or any similar medium anywhere in the world." Given the LEGO resale market is largely online, this provision could effectively shut down any future business activity in this category globally.

Item 17(r) (p. 58)
Significant

Transfer fee jumped from $5,000 to $30,000 — a 500% increase since 2023

The 2023 audited financials cited a $5,000 transfer fee. The 2026 FDD shows the transfer fee is now $30,000 — plus a mandatory $10,000 training fee for the transferee, plus potential broker referral fees. Total exit cost on a sale could approach $40,000+, materially impacting resale value and exit flexibility. BAM also retains a right of first refusal to match any offer.

Notable — financial performance context
Notable

Bottom quartile stores averaging only $293K revenue — breakeven viability questionable

The 4th quartile of 39 stores averaged $293,524 revenue in 2025. With mandatory fees of ~10% of revenue (~$29K), typical occupancy, staffing, and COGS, stores in the bottom quartile likely operate at a loss or near breakeven. The lowest reported revenue was $188,084 — at 6% royalty that's $11,285 to the franchisor before a single employee is paid.

Item 19 — Table 2 (p. 62)
Notable

Gross margin data only available for 104 of 153 stores — incomplete picture

Item 19 Tables 3 and 4 cover only 104 of the 153 qualifying stores. The FDD notes that stores excluded "did not report gross margin in a reliable manner." The bottom quartile gross margin average was just $97,688 — and 4th quartile gross margin percentage averaged 53%, implying thin absolute dollars after COGS, with all operating expenses still to come.

Item 19 — Tables 3–4 (p. 62–63)
Notable

Washington State Attorney General action on no-poach clauses — 2019

BAM entered into an Assurance of Discontinuance with Washington's AG in 2019 over no-poach provisions in franchise agreements that restricted franchisees from hiring each other's workers. BAM denied wrongdoing but agreed to remove and not enforce these clauses. While resolved, it signals early-stage governance issues and the need to monitor labor practice compliance as the system scales.

Item 3 — Litigation (p. 13)
Notable

Franchisor not contractually obligated to defend your trademark rights

Item 13 states: "We are not obligated by the Franchise Agreement to protect any rights granted to you to use the Marks or participate in your defense, protect you against claims of infringement or unfair competition with respect to them." BAM says it intends to defend "as a matter of corporate policy" — but that is discretionary, not contractual. Given the supplemental register status, this is particularly concerning.
